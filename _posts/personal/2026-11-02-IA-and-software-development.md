At the time of this writing technology is kind of wild. Until few months technology has been a warranty carrier. I mean, if you get a work in technology you are likely to have good income, regardless or you tech skills.  Of course, if you are a great software developer then big companies will pay for you even more. 
With the artificial intelligence (AI) arrival, in specific [Large Language Models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) (LLM) along with [Agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent), the comfort zone is not longer that comfy. 

Initially, we got a chat bot, one that can easily pass [Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test), and the one that could easily provide confidently responses, regardless they were or were not correct or accurate. Of course, when you ask about a complex or unknown topic, then you get a answer that you can trust most of the time, and that is the part that create some skepticism. 

I remember about 4 years ago, I use LLMs to ask questions and generate code, as for instance I needed to implement a Base64 encoder and decoder routine in assembler for x86 using AT&T Syntax syntax (when I majored we used Intel Syntax). Anyway, if you kind of understand assembler it was not such a complex task, it was simply being cautious on allocations and how to receive input and return result. I was really surprised when the LLM coded a functional routine. Same year I used to perform some cryptography work, I must admit I couldn't resolve it by myself. The LLM provided an answer and I tried to follow up, which ended up letting know about a framework in python to resolve equations (see [Z3Py Guide](https://ericpony.github.io/z3py-tutorial/guide-examples.htm)) 

Then after, I my peers said that AI was not yet ready for coding, at that time [Devin](https://devin.ai/) was announced, and to be fair it was not ready. Although four years later their pretty is much better, but not main stream as their competitors Claud (from Anthropic), Codex (from OpenAI), Grok (from X),  Gemini (from Google/Alphabet), among many others. 

At this time, I do not think about a bleak future, but a uncertain future. Certainly software development has changed, in a away that it cannot go back to what it was used to. However, if the goal is to free people from understanding the complexity that product creation involves, this particular goals is still a bit far. From my point of view writing code is not any longer a complex task, as so related coding/computer tasks, such as reverse engineering. People with no technical knowledge can now ask their agents to produce software and apply reverse engineering to software.  Technical people who understands very well context and also have integrated properly the AI tools thru some good [hardness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_harness)  can now produce what it used to take multiple worker hours in some minutes. 

The *coder* work is redefining itself, whether or not will be wipeout, it is still to be defined on the AI progress. Software developer now needs to know how to integrate AI in their workflow, as well as many other careers (because not only software development is being affected by the AI). Workers affected by AI needs to allocate AI and being able produce with it, giving value to business.
